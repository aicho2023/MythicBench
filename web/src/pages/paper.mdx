# MythicBench: can a language model lead a dungeon group it has never seen?

<p className="lede">A draft write-up. The structure is final; the results sections fill in from the official v1 run, and every figure below reads the same dataset as the leaderboard.</p>

## 1. The question

Most agent benchmarks ask a model to finish a task once. Raid leaders do something else: they walk a
group into a fight nobody has seen, watch it go wrong, work out why from the combat log, and call a
better plan for the next pull. MythicBench measures that loop. One model gives the orders for a
five-person party through a short Mythic+-style dungeon. It starts blind, and after every wipe it reads a
factual debrief, rewrites its notes, and tries again.

Two things are scored: how well it plays, and how much it improves between attempts.

## 2. The dungeon

The Gravecrown Crypt runs like a Mythic+ key: walk in, clear two trash packs, then kill Mortisar, the
Hollow King, all on one 4:30 timer.

- **Chapel pack.** Four Bonewalkers and a Bone Chanter, whose Grave Volley hits the whole party and can be interrupted
- **Ossuary pack.** Three Bonewalkers and a Marrow Mender, whose Mend Bones heals the pack. Kill the Mender first
- **Mortisar.** An interruptible party-wide cast (Requiem of Bones), a tank buster (Soul Rend), pools under players (Desecrate), adds, and a hard enrage

### Normal and +10

**Normal** has enemies at 60% of their listed health and damage and no affixes; a competent group
should clear it. **+10** has full damage, 85% health, and three affixes, which the party always sees, as
in the game:

- **Restless Graves.** A dead undead leaves a grave for 12 s that heals undead and hurts players standing in it
- **Marrowburst.** Every undead death stacks damage over time on the whole party, so many kills at once hurt
- **Hollow Tyrant.** Mortisar summons adds three times and Desecrates four players instead of three

### Scoring a pull

Each pull scores 0 to 100. A wipe earns up to 70 for progress (each pack 15%, Mortisar 70%). A clear
earns 70 plus up to 30 for speed, with the 10 s revive penalties counted. Win or loss alone would tie
every group that wins and every group that wipes; the score keeps separating them.

## 3. The harness and equal settings

The simulator is deterministic: the same seed and the same orders give a byte-identical replay on any
machine. Every model sees the same JSON observation a scripted bot sees, from each character's point of
view, and returns a priority list of abilities per character every few seconds of game time.

Every model in the official run gets the same settings:

- the same prompt and the same observation format;
- one shared reasoning effort (`medium`);
- the same seeds, call interval and call budget per pull;
- every reply recorded, so any run can be replayed exactly, for free.

## 4. The protocol

- **Blind.** The party knows its own abilities and the affixes, nothing about the enemies
- **Episodes.** Three per model and tier, each up to five pulls, stopping at the first clear
- **Debrief.** After a wipe: who died to what, what hit hardest, every enemy cast seen and whether it was interrupted
- **Notes.** The model rewrites its team notes (about 350 words) and they go into the rules for the next pull
- **New seed each pull.** The mechanics stay the same; the timings shuffle, so memorizing doesn't help
- **Controls.** The two cheapest models also play +10 with memory off, their notes wiped between pulls

An episode's progression score is the mean of its pull scores, with a clear carried forward for the
pulls it saved. The MythicBench score is the mean of the Normal and +10 progression scores; its 95%
interval combines the two tiers' standard errors.

## 7. The scripted bots as calibration

Before any model plays, three hand-written bots check that the benchmark can tell good play from bad:
**expert** (clean pulls, casters first, every kick), **competent** (a few missed kicks and slow dodges)
and **sloppy**. Over 200 seeds on +10 they score 73.5, 59.6 and 16.3, with non-overlapping 95%
intervals, and clear 60%, 13% and 0% of the time. On Normal, expert and competent clear nearly always.
The bots also appear on the leaderboard, scored on the same seeds as the models.
