# MythicBench: can a language model lead a dungeon group it has never seen?

<p className="lede">A draft write-up. The structure is final; the results sections fill in from the official v1 run, and every figure below reads the same dataset as the leaderboard.</p>

## 1. The question

Most agent benchmarks ask a model to finish a task once. Raid leaders do something else: they walk a
group into a fight nobody has seen, watch it go wrong, work out why from the combat log, and call a
better plan for the next pull. MythicBench measures that loop. Five agents, one per character and all
the same model, play a five-person party through a short Mythic+-style dungeon. They start blind. After
every wipe they read the same factual combat log, talk it over, each rewrite their own notes, and try again.

Two things are scored: how well it plays, and how much it improves between attempts.

## 2. The dungeon

The Gravecrown Crypt runs like a Mythic+ key: walk in, clear two trash packs, then kill Mortisar, the
Hollow King, all on one 4:15 timer.

- **Chapel pack.** Four Bonewalkers and a Bone Chanter, whose Grave Volley hits the whole party and can be interrupted
- **Ossuary pack.** Three Bonewalkers and a Marrow Mender, whose Mend Bones heals the pack. Kill the Mender first
- **Mortisar.** An interruptible party-wide cast (Requiem of Bones), a tank buster (Soul Rend), pools under players (Desecrate), adds, and a hard enrage

### Normal and +10

**Normal** has enemies at 95% of their listed health and 90% of their damage, and no affixes; a
competent group should clear it. **+10** has 85% health, 115% damage, and three affixes, which the party
always sees, as in the game:

- **Restless Graves.** A dead undead leaves a grave for 12 s that heals undead and hurts players standing in it
- **Marrowburst.** Every undead death stacks damage over time on the whole party, so many kills at once hurt
- **Hollow Tyrant.** Mortisar summons adds three times, Desecrates four players instead of three, and every
  ~24 s brands a player with Grave Mark, which detonates on every ally within 6 m

### Scoring a pull

Each pull scores 0 to 100. A wipe earns up to 70 for progress (each pack 15%, Mortisar 70%). A kill
earns 70 plus up to 30 for speed, with the 10 s revive penalties counted. Only a kill within the timer
counts as a clear; a kill after it is a depleted key: it scores 70, and the group goes again. Win or loss alone would tie
every group that wins and every group that wipes; the score keeps separating them.

## 3. The harness and equal settings

The simulator is deterministic: the same seed and the same orders give a byte-identical replay on any
machine. Each agent sees what its character would: party frames (everyone's health, position, casts and
auras), its own cooldowns, the enemies, the pools on the ground, and the party chat. It is asked for its
character's orders every 4 seconds of game time, and sooner when something concerns it: a cast it could
interrupt, a pool near it, an ally dying. It replies with a move, a priority list of its own abilities,
and optionally a line in party chat. The simulation pauses while the agents think, so latency never matters.

Every model in the official run gets the same settings:

- the same prompt and the same observation format, and no provider-specific JSON mode;
- one shared reasoning effort (`medium`);
- the same seeds, call interval and call budget per pull;
- every reply recorded, so any run can be replayed exactly, for free.

## 4. The protocol

- **Blind.** The party knows its own abilities and the affixes, nothing about the enemies
- **Episodes.** Three per model and tier, each up to five pulls, stopping at the first clear
- **Combat log.** After a wipe every agent gets the same summary: who died to what, what hit hardest, every enemy cast seen and whether it was interrupted
- **Party talk.** The agents speak in turn, tank first, until all five say they're ready (at most three rounds)
- **Notes.** Each agent then rewrites its own private notes (at most 350 words), which go into its rules for the next pull. Nothing tells the agents what to learn or how
- **New seed each pull.** The mechanics stay the same; the timings shuffle, so memorizing doesn't help
- **Controls.** The two cheapest models also play with memory off: no talk and no notes between pulls

An episode's progression score is the mean of its pull scores, with a clear carried forward for the
pulls it saved. The MythicBench score is the +10 progression score; Normal is a sanity tier, shown but not
ranked on, because strong models clear it on the first pull and it would compress the differences. Intervals are
Student-t 95% intervals over episodes (with three episodes the multiplier is 4.3, not 1.96). The learning
effect is the progression score with memory on minus the score with memory off, on the same seeds: a
within-episode gain alone is biased upward, because a clear carries forward.

## 7. Baselines: the scripted commander and the bots

The agents don't press every button themselves: between calls each character follows its standing orders,
and two shortcuts do the walking ("safe" steps out of pools, "next" walks the route). How much of a score
is the harness and how much is the model? The **scripted commander** answers that. It plays through
exactly the same interface as the agents, reading the same text and replying with the same orders, with
fixed, obvious rules: kick every caster, kill casters first, then adds, then the boss; step out of pools;
the healer heals the lowest ally; walk on when the party is healthy. It never learns. With no orders at
all the party never moves and scores near 0; the commander's rules are the real floor. Over 16 seeds it
scores 55.9 on +10 and 67.6 on Normal, and never clears within the timer. A model has to beat it for its
own decisions to count.

Before any model plays, three hand-written bots check that the benchmark can tell good play from bad:
**expert** (clean pulls, casters first, every kick), **competent** (a few missed kicks and slow dodges)
and **sloppy**. Over 200 seeds on +10 they score 66.1, 49.7 and 7.0, with non-overlapping 95%
intervals, and clear 25%, 1% and 0% of the time. On Normal they clear 99%, 79% and 0%.
The bots also appear on the leaderboard, scored on the same seeds as the models.
